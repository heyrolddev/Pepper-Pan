# Pepper Pan — the mark

Nothing in here is wired into the website. `brand/` is not `public/`, so Next
cannot serve these files even by accident; they are here so the work is not
lost, not because anything is using them.

## What it is

The shop's pan, with a face. Noodles lifting out of it, peppercorns tumbling
in.

The three references — Jollibee, McDonald's, KFC — are three different
strategies. A character, a letterform, a person. This is the first, because
that is the one children attach to, and because the shop is already called a
pan: the object in the name is the character, so nobody has to be taught the
connection.

The peppercorns are not decoration either. The existing wordmark already has a
cluster of them under the letters, so the mark promotes something the brand
owns rather than introducing something new to remember.

The SVGs are the originals — they stay sharp printed at any size, on a
tarpaulin or on a sticker. Use the PNGs where something won't take an SVG
(Facebook, most print shops, Canva).

## Rules that matter

**Give it room.** Keep clear space around the badge of at least the width of
the handle. Crowded against text it stops reading as a mark and starts
reading as a picture.

**Small sizes use the badge, not the character.** Below about 48px the pan
alone loses its edge against a light background. The cream field inside the
badge is what keeps it legible as a favicon.

**On red, use the badge.** A dark pan directly on the brand red is low
contrast — that is exactly what the cream field is for.

**One colour means one colour.** `mascot-mono.svg` has no gradients and no
mid-tones, because the shop's receipt printer is one bit per pixel: every
grey becomes either solid black or nothing. The eyes and smile are knocked
out to paper rather than painted, which is why they survive.

**Don't re-draw the face.** The eyes, the cheeks and the open smile are the
whole character. Changing their spacing is what makes a mascot look like a
copy of itself.

## Still to decide

- **A name.** "Pan-Pan" is easy for a child to say and doubles the shop's own
  name. It could also just be Pepper Pan himself, the way Mr. Peanut is
  Planters. Worth asking a few customers' kids.
- **Poses.** One mark is a logo; a mascot needs a small set — waving,
  holding a tray, thumbs up, sleeping for a closed sign. Those come after
  the face is settled.
